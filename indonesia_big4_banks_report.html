01 · Executive Summary

Pair-trade the cycle: BMRI/BBRI long, BBCA/BBNI hold.

After the BI Rate's surprise +50bp hike to 5.25% in May, the next 12 months reward banks with structural NIM resilience and clean asset quality. Q1 2026 numbers crystallize a 4-way divergence — and the valuation gap between the highest-ROE name (BBCA, 24%) and the lowest (BBNI, 12%) is not reflected in the P/B spread.

Investment thesis (one line each)

BBCAQuality compounder fully priced. 24.1% ROE and 26.95% CAR are best-in-class — but a 3.0× P/B already pays for them. Holding through cycle, not adding.

BMRIMost underpriced top-tier compounder. +16.6% YoY net profit, 0.98% NPL, and 19% ROE for 7.2× P/E and 1.4× P/B. Pays you a 9.7% dividend to wait. Top pick.

BBRIIncome with a thesis. 11.3% yield is real and covered; LAR has fallen from 6.6% to 4.8% YoY — the microfinance recovery is happening. Higher-beta long.

BBNICheap for reason. Sub-book P/B 0.88× reflects 12% ROE, 3.5–3.8% NIM compression, and 20% loan growth that may stress credit costs. Hold pending Wondr-driven CASA mix proof.

Key catalysts (next 12 months)

  • BI rate path — Market consensus expects 4.75–5.25% range. A reversal to 4.75% in 2H26 would lift Big-4 NIM by ~10–20bp on average.
  • FY25 dividend declarations — BMRI (Mar 2026 ex-date already past), BBRI distribute on Apr–May 2026 schedule. Sustainability of BBRI's 91% payout is the swing factor.
  • BBNI Wondr KPI disclosure — Active users + transaction value milestones. If retail CASA mix shifts past 70%, NIM trajectory stabilizes.
  • BMRI deconsolidation effects — Post-BRIS, NIM guidance cut to 4.5–4.7%. Watch 2Q26 for normalized run-rate.

Major risks

  • Rupiah depreciation forcing further BI hikes — May 2026 50bp shock signals BI's tolerance for orthodoxy. Another 50–75bp would compress sector NIM 30–50bp.
  • Micro/SME credit stress at BBRI — Q1 cost-of-credit at upper bound of guidance (3.2% vs 2.9–3.2% FY range). If LAR re-accelerates, BBRI thesis breaks.
  • Digital bank disruption — Allo, Seabank, Jago continuing to compete on deposit pricing. CASA defense at BBCA/BMRI now structural challenge.
  • Election-cycle fiscal slippage — Prabowo administration's Rp 4,200T MBG program may pressure rupiah; bond yields up = NIM benefit but mark-to-market loss on AFS portfolios.
02 · Indonesia Banking Industry Overview

Solid macro, but the cycle has tightened.

Indonesia entered 2026 with 5.6% GDP growth and inflation safely inside BI's 2.5%±1% target. Then the rupiah came under pressure from US rate-volatility and BI surprised with a 50bp hike — pivoting from the 2026 easing path it had telegraphed in January. For banks this means NIM headwinds tempered by sustained 9–10% system loan growth.

How the macro reads through to bank P&Ls

Rate path is the swing variable. The May hike compresses NIM in the short term — banks reprice deposits faster than loans (asset yields are partially fixed-rate corporate exposure). BBNI's published NIM revision (3.8%→3.5–3.8%) is the canary. BBCA's deep CASA franchise (85.2%) gives it the most insulation; BMRI (71.6%) and BBNI (65.6% in 3Q25) are mid-pack.

Growth tailwind intact. Q1 2026 GDP at 5.6% — the strongest print since 2021 — supports the 8–12% credit growth target. BBRI's microfinance and BBNI's corporate loan books both showed >13% YoY growth in Q1. The OJK June 2025 banking statistics report system NPL gross at 2.28% with net NPL 0.86% — credit quality stable but with SME stress noted.

Digital threat is structural, not cyclical. Digital banks (Bank Jago, Seabank, Allo Bank) continue gaining low-cost-funds share. The Big-4's defensive moat is the depth of transaction banking relationships (cash management, payroll, payment rails), not pricing. BCA's 27M customers and 85% CASA show how that moat works in practice.

03 · Business Overview per Bank

Four scale advantages, four different business models.

The Big-4 occupy distinct strategic positions: BBCA is the private-bank deposit franchise, BMRI is the universal-bank market leader, BBRI is the microfinance specialist, BBNI is the corporate/international bank pivoting to consumer digital.

BBCABank Central Asia

Segments: Retail banking-dominant; consumer credit, SME, corporate, treasury, with subsidiaries in syariah (BCA Syariah), multifinance (BCA Finance), and insurance.

Moat: Lowest cost-of-funds in Indonesia. 85.2% CASA ratio means roughly 8 of every 10 deposit rupiah pay near-zero interest. Translates to structural NIM advantage even in tight cycles. 27M+ customers, market-leading mobile (myBCA, BCA mobile).

Strategic initiatives: "Mobile-First" digital strategy; ecosystem partnerships; QRIS dominance. The CASA franchise is so deep that BBCA's 1Q26 deposit growth (+8.3% YoY) is funded entirely by low-cost deposits — CASA grew +11.2% YoY.

BMRIBank Mandiri

Segments: Indonesia's largest universal bank by assets. Balanced exposure across wholesale (corporate, commercial), retail consumer, SME, syariah (until BRIS deconsolidation Q1 2026), and value chain financing.

Moat: Scale economics + SOE relationships. Government-owned 60%+ — anchors infrastructure project financing and SOE cash management. CASA at 71.6% supports moderate NIM.

Strategic initiatives: Livin' by Mandiri (super-app, 25M+ users); Kopra by Mandiri (wholesale); aggressive corporate loan growth (+13.4% FY25). 1Q26 BRIS deconsolidation reset the equity story — pure-bank franchise now clearer.

BBRIBank Rakyat Indonesia

Segments: Microfinance world leader. Ultra Micro Holding (UMi) integrates BRI with PNM and Pegadaian — serves Indonesia's 30M+ micro and ultra-micro borrowers. Also SME, retail, corporate.

Moat: 9,000+ branch network, 90,000+ touchpoints (mantri agents, mobile banking units) — unmatched rural distribution. Translates to highest NIM in the system at 7.9% in 1Q26 vs ~5% peer average.

Strategic initiatives: BRImo digital platform; Senyum Mobile; merchant ecosystem (BRILink). Underwriting discipline tightened post-2024 — LAR down from 6.57% to 4.80% in 1Q26 demonstrates the recovery.

BBNIBank Negara Indonesia

Segments: Corporate-heavy SOE bank historically. Now pivoting to retail/digital. Overseas branches in New York, London, Tokyo, Singapore, Hong Kong, Seoul, Amsterdam support trade finance.

Moat: International franchise + SOE corporate banking. Weakest of the four on deposit franchise (CASA ~66% in 3Q25, lowest among peers). Pivot to digital via Wondr is unproven.

Strategic initiatives: Wondr by BNI (consumer super-app launched 2024; 5.4M users by YE25) and BNIdirect (wholesale, 173K users). USD 700M AT1 issuance April 2026 strengthens capital ahead of aggressive 20% YoY loan growth trajectory.

Interpretation

ROE. BBCA is the only bank to never break above 25% but also never below 16% in five years — the textbook compounder. BMRI is the most cyclical, ranging 11.8%→22.5%→20.7%. BBNI never returned to pre-COVID ~15% until 2022 and is now trending back toward 14%. BBRI hovers stably around 18%.

NIM. BBRI's microfinance moat is durable — peaked at 8% in 2022 and held. BBCA's NIM has been remarkably stable at 5.4–5.9%. BBNI's NIM is on a clear downtrend: 4.7%→4.1% over five years. This is the structural problem with the BBNI thesis.

NPL. BMRI's NPL went from 3.1% to 1.1% in five years — best-in-class trajectory. BBNI similarly improved from 4.4% to 2.0%. BBRI's NPL has held steady at ~2.8–3.0% reflecting its higher-risk microfinance book.

05 · Valuation Analysis

The ROE-to-P/B map shows the mispricing.

In a normal sell-side regression, P/B should rise linearly with sustainable ROE. Plotting current valuations against trailing ROE reveals BBNI as fairly priced for its profitability, BMRI as significantly under-priced, BBRI as fair-to-cheap, and BBCA as priced for perfection.

Where the premium is justified

BBCA's 3.0× P/B is high but defensible: 24% ROE with the lowest cost-of-funds franchise in the system, 27% CAR, and beta of just 0.02. Investors are paying for crisis-resistance. The premium narrows only if NIM erodes structurally past 5.0%.

BMRI's 1.4× P/B looks like a clear bargain. At 19% ROE — second-highest in the group — fair P/B by the residual-income model is closer to 2.0×. The discount may reflect post-BRIS deconsolidation noise; we expect it to narrow as 2Q26 clean numbers come in.

BBRI's 1.7× P/B is in line with its 17.5% ROE. Yield is the kicker — 11.3% covered by stable cash flows is meaningfully above Indonesia's 10-year government bond yield of ~7%. Investors get equity exposure at bond-like income.

BBNI's 0.88× P/B is a value-trap signal. At 12% ROE and declining NIM trajectory, fair P/B is ~1.0× — i.e., the stock is roughly fair, not cheap. The "discount to book" framing only works if you believe ROE will recover toward 15%+, which requires the Wondr/digital pivot to work.

07 · Key Risks

Five risk vectors to monitor.

Medium

Macroeconomic / FX

Rupiah pressure forced BI's +50bp May hike. Further hikes would compress NIM 30–50bp across sector. Mitigant: BBCA's CASA insulates it; BBRI's microfinance NIM is structurally above policy rate.

High

Interest rate

Asymmetric: banks reprice deposits faster than loans on the way up; reverse on the way down. BBNI's 50bp credit-yield decline in Q1 is the visible damage. Highest exposure: BBNI > BMRI > BBRI > BBCA.

Medium

Credit quality

BBRI cost-of-credit at 3.2% (upper bound of FY guidance). BBNI LAR 8.6% with rapid 20% loan growth — vintage risk. BMRI/BBCA NPLs are at historical lows, providing cushion.

Medium

Digital disruption

Bank Jago, Seabank, Allo Bank competing for low-cost deposits. CASA mix erosion of 200–300bp over 3 years possible if competition intensifies. Largest threat to BBNI's already-weak CASA franchise.

Medium

Regulatory

OJK Buy-Now-Pay-Later regulation (effective 2026); potential SOE dividend payout floors; M&A constraints; possible BPJS subsidy revision affecting consumer borrowing power. SOE banks (BMRI, BBRI, BBNI) most exposed.

Low–Med

Capital adequacy

All four banks well-capitalized. BBCA's 27% CAR provides extreme buffer; BBNI just issued USD 700M AT1 (April 2026) to support 20% loan growth. No bank near regulatory floor (8% Tier-1 + buffers).

08 · Final Recommendation

Four picks for four mandates.

Best Overall Pick
BMRIBank Mandiri
BUY · TP 5,600

19% ROE for 1.37× P/B is the cleanest value-quality combination in Indonesia. +16.6% Q1 net profit growth, 0.98% NPL, 9.7% dividend yield. Post-BRIS noise should clear by 2Q26 — entry point.

Strongest Defensive
BBCABank Central Asia
HOLD · TP 6,500

Fortress balance sheet (27% CAR), highest ROE (24%), lowest beta (0.02). The bank you buy if you're concerned about Indonesian macro tail risk. Already priced for quality — limited upside but compounds quietly.

Strongest Growth
BBRIBank Rakyat Indonesia
BUY · TP 3,800

Microfinance NIM at 7.9% is the system's highest. LAR recovery from 6.6% to 4.8% shows underwriting discipline working. 11.3% dividend yield while the recovery plays out.

Most Undervalued (on book)
BBNIBank Negara Indonesia
HOLD · TP 4,000

P/B 0.88× appears cheap but the 12% ROE justifies most of the discount. Buy only if you believe Wondr/digital pivot will lift ROE back to 15%+. We need 2–3 quarters of CASA mix improvement before upgrading.

Portfolio construction view

For income-driven mandates: 50% BMRI + 30% BBRI + 20% BBCA. Blended yield ~8.5%, blended ROE ~20%, balanced across SOE/private exposure.

For quality-driven mandates: 50% BBCA + 30% BMRI + 20% BBRI. Blended ROE ~22%, lowest aggregate beta. Sacrifices yield for stability.

For value-tilted mandates: 40% BMRI + 30% BBRI + 30% BBNI. Blended P/B ~1.3×, blended yield ~10%. Highest beta to a cycle recovery / rate-cut scenario.

Skip BBNI in defensive positioning until the digital franchise proves it can structurally shift CASA mix above 70%.
